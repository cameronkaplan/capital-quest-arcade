# Mini-Game Content Ideas

Use this file as the approval queue before adding more scripted Court cases or recording more Capital Call-In audio. Road Trip ideas are included for traceability; the first batch can be implemented directly.

## Capital Call-In Script Bank

Style target: 4-8 seconds, one caller, one joke or memory hook, no answer text shown in the on-screen prompt until choices appear. Some calls ask for the state; others ask for the capital.

## Weird Neighbor Court Approval Queue

Use safe case names in the app. The title should not name the answer. The testimony can name a clue state, but should avoid saying the answer directly.

### West
- The Coastal Roadblock: A west-coast state wants to visit the top coast state, but one coastal middle state blocks the trip.
- The Lower Box Complaint: The upper rectangle says the lower rectangle keeps copying its shape.
- The Four Corners Traffic Jam: A desert state says only one corner-touching neighbor leads toward the Rockies.
- The Potato Witness: The big mountain state says a narrow neighbor is tucked along its western face.
- The Desert Detour: A giant western state wants to reach the Grand Canyon state but must pass a desert neighbor first.
- The Island Flight Mix-Up: The far north state says the island state cannot be reached by regular road trip.
- The Salt Stop: A western state says the route from Nevada to Colorado needs a salty middle stop.
- The C-O-W Coast Order: The coast states argue about who is middle in the C-O-W stack.

### Midwest
- The Lower Twin: The upper Dakota says the other Dakota is directly below.
- The I-State Pileup: Three I-states argue about which one touches the Great Lakes.
- The Rectangle Ladder: Nebraska says the rectangle to it's south is blocking the way.
- The Mitten Witness: The mitten state says a neighbor across the lake gets mistaken for it.
- The River Crossing: Missouri says one northern neighbor is the bridge toward Minnesota.
- The Not-St-Louis Objection: A state says its famous city is not the capital and wants the paperwork returned.
- The Corn Belt Shortcut: Kansas says its route to the Dakotas must go through this corn state first.
- The Great Lakes Lost Case: Wisconsin and Michigan argue over which one sits west of Lake Michigan.

### Southeast
- The Mixed-Up Sidekick: Alabama says its western neighbor keeps being confused with it.
- The MAG Line: Three states argue about the west-to-east order of Mississippi, Alabama, and Georgia.
- The Panhandle Problem: Florida says a northern neighbor is the gateway before the long skinny peninsula.
- The Chef MIMAL Complaint: Louisiana says it is the boot under the MIMAL stack.
- The New Oklahoma Transfer: Oklahoma says it moved into Southeast practice and wants Arkansas to stop acting surprised.
- The Carolina Mirror: North Carolina says the southern Carolina is directly below it.
- The River Baton Case: Louisiana says one eastern neighbor is the first step toward Alabama.
- The Mountain Corner: West Virginia says Kentucky and Virginia both crowd its southern edge.
- The Texas Border Blocker: Texas says one state blocks the path toward Arizona.
- The Tennessee Top Rail: Tennessee says it stretches across the top of several southern neighbors.

### Northeast
- The Look-Alike Neighbor: New Hampshire says its look-alike neighbor sits west.
- The Tiny State Stack: Rhode Island says it is small but still knows which larger neighbor surrounds it.
- The Maine Touch Rule: New Hampshire says it is the only state in the pair that touches Maine.
- The Jersey Bridge: New York says one neighbor is the gateway toward Delaware.
- The Pennsylvania Detour: New Jersey says a westward road quickly becomes a Pennsylvania problem.
- The Chesapeake Shortcut: Maryland says Delaware is small but blocks a coastal route.
- The New England Ladder: Connecticut says the path north runs through Massachusetts before New Hampshire or Maine.
- The Vermont West Case: Vermont says west means left on the map, even when the states look similar.

## Road Trip Inventory
